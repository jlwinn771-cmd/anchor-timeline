The Anchor Timeline

One timeline, five eras, and a presidential election every four years. Each lecture adds dated events to it, each assessment asks students to add more, and at the end of the course every student builds it from a blank page.

Lectures build itEach lecture places 2–5 events on the class timeline and teaches one timeline skill.
Students add to itFor each era, students research and add three events that lecture did not cover.
Students build itThe final exam is a blank line from 1492 to 1877. Students draw the eras and place the anchors from memory.

The election rail

An election every four years

Federal politics is the backbone of the timeline. Students learn the four-year rhythm of presidential elections from 1788 to 1876 and know the eight pivotal elections well. Social, economic, and cultural events are placed against that rhythm.

Era Checkpoints

25%
4 in class · Weeks 4, 6, 11, 14 · 20 minutes
  • Mark the era's start and end years and say why the era begins and ends there.
  • Place the era's anchor dates and presidential elections on a strip.
  • Explain one cause-and-effect link in two or three sentences.

Reconstruction has no checkpoint. Its anchors are tested on the final.
